- **What:** the policy is frozen at the seed's pilot, and a fresh critic learns from that pilot's
  games (`--set critic.warmup=true`).
- **Budget, pre-registered:** **12 billed box-hours.** At the learner's ≈ 110K hanchan/h that is
  ≈ 0.4M hanchan, inside `05` §4's 0.3–0.5M. `watch --budget-box-hours 12` takes the `full`
  snapshot, which becomes the P1 run's `--init`, and downs the fleet.
- **Acceptance, judged on the trainer's diagnostics over the last third of B0:**
  1. calibration (10 bins) monotone in the final window;
  2. explained variance overall and in the last kyoku (`critic/explained_variance_last_kyoku`)
     not falling. Plateaued or rising both count.
- **If either fails:** extend once by 4 box-hours, continuing from the snapshot. If it still
  fails, stop and report: D52's reopen condition.

## Learner check (P1's tuning allowance, `05` §2.2; registered 2026-10-11, before any timing)

- **Why:** B0's learner drained only ≈ 45K hanchan/h with 4 loader workers, against P0's ≈ 110K/h
  and the ≈ 110K/h two actor boxes produce. The critic's oracle featurization is the likely cost.
  If more loader workers raise the rate, P1 trains on more games in the same box-hours.
- **What is tuned:** only the learner's loader workers (`dataset.num_workers`). That is data
  loading only, so what P1 learns from a given drain is unchanged. Learning hyperparameters, batch
  sizes and the fleet shape stay as registered.
- **Where:** its own rented learner box (fleet run `p1-tune`, no actors), outside both runs' 432
  box-hours; ≤ 2 box-hours (≈ $1).
- **Command:** `tools/fleet/bench_learner.py --arm P1 --games 3200 --versions 4 --workers 4 8 16
  --no-compile`. This is P1's learner path as `train_online.py` runs it: the oracle loader in
  chunks of 50 logs, GAE on the learner, then PPO and a b8c128 critic in one backward. It runs
  eager, as P0's check did (its amendment). The 3,200-log drain is close to B0's average drain
  (≈ 4,900 logs).
- **Rule:** the fastest `full` samples/s wins, and a count within 5% of the fastest loses to fewer
  workers (P0's rule). Added for P1: a count is eligible only if its `full` pass peaks below 32 GB
  (PSS of the benchmark and its workers). That is half the fleet's 64 GB RAM floor, since a
  replacement learner can be any box at the floor.
- **Applied** to both runs with `--set dataset.num_workers=N`.
- **Reported, not acted on:** whether the learner still binds P1 at the chosen count. If it does,
  the actor boxes idle part of the time; that is a cost question for later, not a change now.
- *Amendment, 2026-10-11, after the worker timing and before the thread timing:* a profile of
  the learner's main process (first 1,600 logs, 4 workers) found 45% of its time in
  concatenating each chunk's trajectories, at ≈ 0.4 GB/s. Torch's default of 32 CPU threads in
  the main process competes with the loader workers' threads for the box's 30.7-CPU quota. With
  4 or 8 threads, that step took 5 s instead of 47 s, and the profiled rate rose from 2.4K to
  4.1K and 4.5K samples/s. So the check also tunes **torch CPU threads in the trainer**
  (`control.num_threads`, new, default 0 = torch's default). That is throughput only: these
  CPU ops are copies, concatenation and indexing, with identical results. Command: the same, with
  `--workers 4 --threads 4 8 16`. Rule: the fastest `full` samples/s, and a count within 5% of
  the fastest loses to fewer threads. Applied with `--set control.num_threads=N`.
- **Result, loader workers (2026-10-11; Vast EPYC 9684X, 30.7-CPU quota, 104 GB, RTX 4090;
  3,200-game drain, 510K decisions, eager, torch's default 32 threads;
  `workdir/fleet/runs/p1-tune/p1-learner-workers.json`):**

  | Loader workers | Full, samples/s | Data only, samples/s | Peak memory, full |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **4** | **2,388** | 9,931 | 22.9 GB |
  | 8 | 1,436 | 9,272 | 40.5 GB (over 32) |
  | 16 | 1,538 | 1,301 | 74.6 GB (over 32) |

  GPU only (training steps, eager): 16,002 samples/s. **Choice: 4 workers**, the fastest and
  the only count under the memory ceiling. The loader is ≈ 4× faster than the learner consumes:
  P1's learner is bound by its main process, not by featurization. 2,388 samples/s ≈ 54K
  hanchan/h (≈ 158 decisions per hanchan), close to B0's ≈ 45K/h.
- **Result, trainer threads (2026-10-11, same box and drain, 4 workers;
  `workdir/fleet/runs/p1-tune/p1-learner-threads.json`; profile in `p1-profile.jsonl`):**

  | Torch threads | Full, samples/s | Data only, samples/s | Peak memory, full |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 32 (default; above) | 2,388 | 9,931 | 22.9 GB |
  | 4 | 4,545 | 9,996 | 23.5 GB |
  | **8** | **4,812** | 9,219 | 23.3 GB |
  | 16 | 4,549 | 10,506 | 23.3 GB |

  GPU only: 17,387 samples/s. **Choice: 8 threads.** It is the fastest; 4 is 5.5% slower, outside
  the 5% margin. **P1's learner: 4 workers and 8 threads, ≈ 4.8K samples/s ≈ 110K hanchan/h**
  (eager), twice the default and about what two actor boxes produce. Check cost: $0.60 (one box,
  1.3 h).
- **The first attempt deadlocked** (also locally): the benchmark generated its drain in-process,
  and loader workers forked after the arena had run sat at 0 CPU on a futex. A rerun on the
  existing drain ran normally, so generation now runs in a child process. P0's check generated
  in-process too, and its "45-minute `torch.compile` hang" ended the same way, on a rerun with
  the drain present. It was probably this deadlock, not compilation.
